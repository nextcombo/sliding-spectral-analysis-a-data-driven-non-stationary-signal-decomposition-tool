# Sliding Spectral Analysis: A Data-Driven Non-Stationary Signal Decomposition Tool [with MATLAB Code]

Abstract:
\tSpectral Analysis of Spontaneous Activity (SASA) is a signal decomposition technique aimed at expanding signals into interpretable and physically meaningful components (e.g., sinusoidal curves, noise, etc.). This paper presents new theoretical and practical results on the separation of SASA from other methods and introduces a novel method called Sliding SASA. Firstly, SASA is combined with unsupervised classification algorithms to provide a fully automated data-driven component extraction method, investigating the limitations of component separation and theoretical research on SASA. Secondly, a detailed automatic SASA method is used to design a sliding analysis window-based approach that provides better results than classical SASA methods when analyzing non-stationary signals with varying number of time-varying components. Finally, it is suggested that compared to Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) and Synchronous Compression Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), the Sliding SASA method is suitable for both synthetic and real-world signals.
Despite efforts to make spectral methods more robust to outliers [17], [18], classical SSA methods fail to correctly reconstruct components when emphasis is placed on non-stationary signals. The grouping method based on singular spectrum analysis becomes ineffective, as each singular vector can only capture a portion of each component. To overcome this limitation, a new algorithm called sliding SSA is introduced, and a frame-based SSA (such as Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT)) is proposed to track the determined components between adjacent frames. This new approach significantly improves results compared to classical SSA and stationary and non-stationary signals. The structure of this document is as follows: the next section briefly reviews the basic SSA algorithms and proposes an unsupervised component grouping method based on hierarchical clustering. Section three studies the separation capability of SSA considering several theoretical and practical aspects through simulation verification. Section four introduces a new algorithm called sliding SSA that surpasses the limitations of classical SSA algorithms when dealing with non-stationary multi-component signals. Section five contributes to the performance comparison among four mode decomposition techniques: SSA, EMD, synchronous compression, and the newly proposed sliding SSA, which is applicable to both synthetic and real-world signals. Finally, in the sixth section, the paper is summarized, and future research directions are discussed.

The SSA method is studied both theoretically and practically, and several enhancement suggestions are proposed for automatic component grouping and non-stationary signal processing. A major contribution is the introduction of a new algorithm called sliding SSA, which enhances the analysis of non-stationary signals due to the STFT with a sliding analysis window. The algorithm is compared with classical SSA methods and other state-of-the-art pattern extraction methods: EMD and synchronous compression methods. Our numerical experiments show that SSA significantly outperforms EMD in many cases, and the sliding SSA can achieve comparable results to the synchronous compression method. In terms of improving the robustness of the new algorithm, better tracking components are obtained. This may lead to the development of more advanced applications for real-world signals.
